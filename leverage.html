Leverage map · 7 chokepoints

Who holds what in the AI supply chain

No middle power can build the whole stack. Several hold one piece that nobody else can easily replace. Read from the mine at the top to the model at the bottom.

Layer Held by What they hold Limit
Critical minerals Australia, Canada, Chile Mines for the metals that chips and data centres need. China dominates the refining. Canada mines but does not refine at scale.
Chip-making machines Netherlands ASML is the only maker of the extreme ultraviolet lithography machines used for the most advanced chips. Researchers question whether it has ever given The Hague real bargaining power.
Chip materials Japan Leading supplier of the specialist chemicals and wafers that fabs depend on. Closely tied to US export policy.
Advanced fabrication Taiwan TSMC makes the large majority of the world's leading-edge chips. Taiwan's diplomatic position keeps it out of most formal coalitions.
AI memory South Korea SK Hynix and Samsung supply most of the high-bandwidth memory inside AI accelerators. Its biggest customers are American chip designers.
Energy and capital United Arab Emirates Money and power for very large data centres, including the Stargate site in Abu Dhabi. The chips inside still need US export approval.
Talent, data and market India A very large engineering workforce, a huge user base, and public digital systems built at national scale. Its compute runs on imported chips.

Holding a chokepoint is not the same as using it

A forthcoming study built on interviews with former US and Dutch officials asks whether ASML gave the Netherlands any real say in the chip export talks. If the strongest chokepoint in the chain confers little, the weaker ones confer less.

Why this matters for a coalition

Each holder is weak alone, because each also depends on the others. The case for acting together is that the pieces add up to something neither superpower can do without.
