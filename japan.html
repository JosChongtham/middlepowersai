Japan

The most AI-friendly rulebook of any large democracy: a law with no fines, a safety institute that builds testing tools, and a chip supply base the whole industry relies on.

Stated position

Tokyo wants Japan to be the most AI-friendly country in the world. Its approach is to promote AI and steer it through guidance, rather than to regulate it with penalties.

It led the G7's Hiroshima AI Process, one of the first international codes of conduct for advanced AI.

Safety institute

The Japan AI Safety Institute opened in February 2024 inside the Information-technology Promotion Agency. It develops evaluation methods and publishes open-source tools for red-teaming.

Japan's AI Basic Plan calls for expanding its staff and its technical evaluation of models.

Laws and rules

The AI Promotion Act passed in May 2025 and took full effect that September. It sets national policy and lets the government name companies that cause harm, but carries no monetary penalties.

A first AI Basic Plan followed in December 2025 and a second in July 2026.

Chips

A leading supplier of the specialist chemicals, wafers and tools that chip plants depend on, which makes it essential to any export-control regime.

A US bill introduced in 2026 would give Japan and the Netherlands 150 days to align their export controls with Washington's.
