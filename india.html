India

It hosted the 2026 AI summit, joined Washington's supply-chain club the same week, and argues that AI governance should be about development as much as danger. No other middle power sits at so many tables at once.

Stated position

India frames AI as a development tool first. Its February 2026 summit was the first in the series held in the Global South, and its declaration speaks of AI for all and of sharing the benefits fairly. The text is voluntary and non-binding, and both the US and China endorsed it.

Critics say the summit moved the agenda away from frontier risk, and that it stopped short of founding a real coalition of middle powers.

Alignment

India signed the Pax Silica Declaration on 20 February 2026, during its own summit. Indian analysts describe this as issue-based alignment: it cuts reliance on China for chips and minerals without committing India on wider foreign policy. China has said it opposes the coalition.

The worry at home is autonomy. One policy brief warns of a "subcontractor trap" in which India supplies talent and markets while others keep control.

Safety institute

The IndiaAI Safety Institute was announced in January 2025 under the national AI mission. It is built as a hub with cells in universities and partner organisations, not as a single lab.

How far it has got is disputed. A June 2026 assessment called it still more aspiration than working body, lacking its own resources, mandate and independence.

Laws and rules

The India AI Governance Guidelines of 5 November 2025 set seven principles and concluded that a separate AI law was not needed for now. The one binding AI-specific rule took effect on 20 February 2026 and requires platforms to label AI-generated content.

In July 2026 the technology ministry was reported to be starting work on a dedicated AI law.

Compute and chips

The IndiaAI Mission, funded at about 1.25 billion US dollars, rents out shared GPUs to startups and researchers at roughly 65 rupees an hour. The pool passed 38,000 GPUs by February 2026 and 45,000 by June.

The weak point is hardware. The pool runs on Nvidia chips that fall under US export controls, and India refines under 2% of the critical minerals it uses.

Home-built models

Three were launched at the summit. Sarvam AI open-sourced models of 30 and 105 billion parameters, and the BharatGen group led by IIT Bombay released a 17 billion parameter model. Both families cover 22 Indian languages.

These are far smaller than frontier systems. The aim is local language and public-service use, not a race at the top.

What to watch

Whether the 100,000 GPU target is met by December 2026.

Whether a draft AI law appears, and how strict it is.

Whether India backs either of the middle-power safety texts before Geneva.

Whether the safety institute gets its own budget and staff.
