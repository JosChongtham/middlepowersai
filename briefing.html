Briefing no. 1 · Executive summary · November 2026

Three offers and a deadline

Two superpowers have their AI clubs. Middle powers are building a third, and Geneva is its test.

Bottom line

States outside the US and China now have three AI tables to choose from. The one middle powers are building for themselves is the least finished, and the Geneva summit in June 2027 will show whether it can speak with one voice.

Five things to know

  1. Washington's table is the biggest. Pax Silica, the US-led supply-chain club for minerals, chips and compute, had 26 signatories by October 2026. India joined in February.
  2. Beijing has built its own. The World AI Cooperation Organization was founded in Shanghai in July by 29 states, including Russia, Brazil and Indonesia. No G7 economy joined.
  3. Middle powers are improvising a third. Four moves in ten weeks: the Finland–Norway call for control of frontier models (September), a renewed UK–Canada security pact (October), the Canada–EU alliance (October) and the Paris Call (November).
  4. Almost nobody picks just one. Germany has signed Pax Silica and the frontier-model call. Kazakhstan is in both of those and in the Shanghai organisation too. Hedging is the norm, not the exception.
  5. India is the swing state to watch. It hosted the 2026 summit and backed Pax Silica, but has not signed either middle-power safety text.

Why it matters

No middle power can build the full AI stack alone, and each depends on technology controlled in Washington or Beijing. Acting together is their main route to a say over rules, access and safety. Today that coalition is a set of overlapping declarations, not an institution, and a mostly voluntary one.

What to watch before Geneva

  • Whether the frontier-model call turns into an actual institution, with members, money and a mandate.
  • Whether India, Brazil or Indonesia sign any middle-power safety text before Geneva.
  • Whether the Canada–EU alliance opens to other countries, as Carney has suggested.
  • Whether US export policy pushes more states towards hedging, or back into line.
