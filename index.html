The short version

What is actually going on

Who counts as a middle power

There is no agreed list. Here it means a state with real leverage in AI, such as chips, compute, talent, data or a large market, that still depends on the most advanced systems being built in the United States or China.

Canada, the UK, Japan, South Korea, Germany, France and Singapore are often named. So are India, Brazil and the UAE.

Why the stakes are rising

The International AI Safety Report 2026 sorts the dangers into three kinds: people using AI to do harm, for example in cyberattacks; systems that fail or slip outside human control; and slower damage to jobs, trust and society.

Middle powers carry a fourth risk of their own. If the technology that runs their economies is controlled abroad, so are the decisions about it.

India and the swing states

India hosted the 2026 AI summit, where 88 countries and organisations, the US and China included, backed the New Delhi Declaration. In the same week it joined Washington's Pax Silica.

Brazil and Indonesia are founding members of the Shanghai organisation. The UAE has signed Pax Silica and the call for control. States like these decide which table fills up.
