What middle powers could build together

The institutional ideas in circulation, who is behind each, and the hardest question each has to answer.

Safety

An international body for frontier AI

An institution that sets standards for the most capable models, makes verification possible, and convenes states when capability thresholds are crossed.

Backed by Finland and Norway, with 20 countries and the European Commission at launch. Welcomed by the UN Secretary-General.

Hardest question The companies it would oversee are American and Chinese, and neither government has signed.

UN statement →
Safety

A stability board for AI, through the G20

Modelled on the Financial Stability Board set up after the 2008 crisis. It could open the way to some mandatory testing and to sharing information on serious incidents.

Backed by A Chatham House commentary of October 2026, which urges the UK to push it.

Hardest question The G20 includes both superpowers and works by consensus.

Chatham House →
Compute

Pooled compute and a public AI utility

National AI labs and supercomputers joined into one consortium that builds models and sells access as a public utility. Canada has proposed pooling its computing with Europe's.

Backed by First floated by the Barcelona Supercomputing Center in 2024. Taken up by Mark Carney in 2026.

Hardest question Scale. Brookings notes the big US firms will spend over 700 billion dollars in 2026, which no coalition can match dollar for dollar.

East Asia Forum →
Bargaining

A coalition of the dependent

States that rely on foreign frontier AI pool what they do hold across the supply chain, and use it jointly to secure access. A coordination mechanism, not a formal alliance.

Backed by An ASPI analysis of September 2026, written from Australia.

Hardest question Whether holding a chokepoint gives real leverage. See the leverage map.

ASPI Strategist →
Political

A middle-power charter

A declared set of shared political and economic goals, so that closer ties rest on more than a string of bilateral deals.

Backed by A Canadian expert group, ahead of the Canada–EU summit in October 2026.

Hardest question Who counts as a middle power, and who gets left out.

Political

A third pole

A standing coalition that offers other states partnership under shared rules, with a common finance platform, as an alternative to joining either superpower's orbit.

Backed by A Global Solutions Initiative policy brief of February 2026.

Hardest question Many candidates already sit inside a US-led or China-led initiative.
